1. Policy Statement

Baimuru Primary School is committed to providing a safe, respectful, inclusive and supportive environment in which every student can learn, develop positive relationships and achieve their potential.

The School believes that positive student behaviour is an essential part of effective teaching and learning.

Behaviour management at Baimuru Primary School is therefore based on the principles of:

  • respect;
  • fairness;
  • consistency;
  • responsibility;
  • positive behaviour;
  • student welfare;
  • inclusion;
  • accountability;
  • restoration and learning; and
  • the protection of students and staff.

The School seeks to manage behaviour in a manner that teaches students appropriate conduct rather than relying solely on punishment.

All behaviour-management practices shall be consistent with applicable Papua New Guinea legislation, National Department of Education policies and directives, provincial education requirements and the approved policies of Baimuru Primary School.

The School's approach is guided particularly by the Behaviour Management Policy for the National Education System of Papua New Guinea, which provides the national framework for improving student behaviour and welfare.

2. Purpose

The purpose of this policy is to establish a consistent framework for managing student behaviour at Baimuru Primary School.

The policy aims to:

  • promote positive behaviour;
  • establish clear expectations for students;
  • maintain a safe and orderly learning environment;
  • support student welfare and wellbeing;
  • provide fair and consistent responses to inappropriate behaviour;
  • clarify the responsibilities of students, teachers, parents and School leadership;
  • provide appropriate responses to serious or repeated misconduct;
  • encourage students to take responsibility for their actions;
  • protect the rights, dignity and safety of students and staff; and
  • strengthen cooperation between the School, parents, guardians and community.

3. Scope

This policy applies to:

  • all students enrolled at Baimuru Primary School;
  • teachers;
  • support and administrative staff where their conduct relates to student behaviour;
  • parents and guardians in matters relating to their children;
  • volunteers and community members involved in School activities; and
  • visitors where their conduct affects the School environment.

The policy applies during:

  • normal School hours;
  • School activities;
  • excursions and educational visits;
  • sporting activities;
  • School functions;
  • activities conducted on behalf of the School; and
  • other circumstances where student behaviour has a direct connection with the School.

Where appropriate, the policy may also apply to serious behaviour occurring outside School that has a significant impact on the safety, welfare or orderly operation of the School.

4. Guiding Principles

4.1 Positive Behaviour

The School will promote and reinforce appropriate behaviour rather than focusing only on misconduct. Students should be taught what appropriate behaviour looks like and be given reasonable opportunities to improve.

4.2 Respect

Every member of the School community has the right to be treated with dignity and respect. Students are expected to respect: teachers; staff; fellow students; parents and guardians; visitors; School property; and the wider community.

4.3 Fairness and Consistency

Behaviour-management responses should be fair, reasonable and consistent. However, consistency does not necessarily mean that every student receives exactly the same response. Relevant circumstances, age, developmental level, seriousness and previous behaviour may be considered.

4.4 Student Welfare

Behaviour management must take account of student welfare. Where inappropriate behaviour may be connected to welfare, family, learning, emotional, social or other difficulties, the School should consider appropriate support and referral.

4.5 Accountability

Students are expected to accept responsibility for their behaviour and understand the consequences of their choices.

4.6 Restoration

Where appropriate, students should be encouraged to repair harm, restore relationships and learn from incidents.

4.7 Inclusion

Behaviour-management practices should recognise the dignity and individual circumstances of students and should not unfairly discriminate against students.

4.8 Safety

The safety of students and staff is a primary consideration in all behaviour-management decisions.

5. Expected Student Behaviour

Students of Baimuru Primary School are expected to:

  • attend School regularly and punctually;
  • participate positively in learning;
  • listen to and follow reasonable instructions;
  • treat teachers and staff with respect;
  • treat other students respectfully;
  • use appropriate language;
  • behave responsibly;
  • care for School property;
  • keep the School environment clean;
  • follow School rules;
  • act safely;
  • respect differences among members of the School community;
  • resolve disagreements appropriately;
  • tell a trusted adult when they are concerned about their own or another person's safety; and
  • represent the School responsibly during School activities.

The School may publish a separate Student Code of Conduct / School Rules providing a more detailed statement of expected behaviour.

6. School-Wide Behaviour Expectations

Baimuru Primary School promotes five broad expectations:

Be Respectful

Students should show respect through their words, actions and treatment of others.

Be Responsible

Students should take responsibility for their learning, behaviour and belongings.

Be Safe

Students should behave in ways that protect themselves and others from harm.

Be Ready to Learn

Students should attend prepared, participate in learning and follow reasonable classroom expectations.

Be a Positive Member of the School Community

Students should contribute positively to the School and community.

These expectations may be adapted into age-appropriate classroom and School rules.

7. Responsibilities

7.1 Students

Students are responsible for: following School rules; following reasonable instructions; treating others respectfully; taking responsibility for their actions; caring for School property; reporting serious safety concerns; participating in behaviour-management processes when required; and making reasonable efforts to improve behaviour.

7.2 Teachers

Teachers are responsible for: establishing clear classroom expectations; teaching and reinforcing appropriate behaviour; supervising students appropriately; responding to inappropriate behaviour fairly; recording significant incidents; communicating relevant concerns to the appropriate School authority; supporting students to improve behaviour; and referring serious or repeated behaviour to the Head Teacher or designated officer.

Teachers should seek to manage ordinary classroom behaviour within their professional responsibilities while recognising when a matter requires further intervention.

7.3 Head Teacher

The Head Teacher is responsible for: providing leadership in behaviour management; ensuring implementation of this policy; supporting teachers; managing serious and repeated behaviour matters; communicating with parents and guardians where necessary; ensuring appropriate records are maintained; ensuring disciplinary responses are reasonable and authorised; referring matters to appropriate authorities where required; and monitoring the effectiveness of the School's behaviour-management system.

7.4 Board of Management

The Board of Management supports the School's overall governance framework and provides appropriate oversight of School policies. The Board may: approve the School's Behaviour Management Policy; support the development of a positive School environment; support appropriate School rules; consider matters referred to it within its authority; and support School-community cooperation.

The Board does not replace the professional and day-to-day responsibilities of teachers and the Head Teacher.

7.5 Parents and Guardians

Parents and guardians are important partners in managing student behaviour. Parents and guardians are encouraged to: support School expectations; discuss behaviour concerns with the School; cooperate with reasonable interventions; attend meetings when requested; support their child in taking responsibility for behaviour; and work with the School to address repeated or serious concerns.

The School will seek to communicate significant behaviour concerns with parents or guardians where appropriate.

8. Positive Behaviour Support

The School will promote positive behaviour through strategies such as:

  • clear expectations;
  • classroom routines;
  • positive reinforcement;
  • recognition of good behaviour;
  • encouragement;
  • student participation;
  • counselling and guidance where available;
  • appropriate teacher-student relationships;
  • communication with parents;
  • student leadership opportunities; and
  • early intervention.

Positive behaviour should be recognised and encouraged as part of normal teaching and School life.

9. Classroom Behaviour Management

Teachers are normally the first level of response to ordinary classroom behaviour.

Teachers may use appropriate classroom strategies including:

  • reminders;
  • redirection;
  • clarification of expectations;
  • private discussion;
  • seating or activity adjustments;
  • restorative discussion;
  • temporary removal from a particular activity where appropriate;
  • communication with parents; and
  • referral to the Head Teacher where behaviour is serious, persistent or beyond the teacher's reasonable classroom-management capacity.

Teachers must exercise professional judgment and consider the age and circumstances of the student.

10. Levels of Misbehaviour

Behaviour may generally be considered at three levels.

Level 1 — Minor Misbehaviour

Examples may include: minor disruption; talking at inappropriate times; failure to follow routine instructions; minor classroom disturbance; inappropriate language of a less serious nature; minor lateness; failure to bring required materials; or other low-level behaviour.

Minor behaviour should normally be addressed by the classroom teacher through appropriate classroom-management strategies.

Level 2 — Repeated or Significant Misbehaviour

Examples may include: repeated disruption; persistent refusal to follow reasonable instructions; repeated disrespect; repeated truancy or serious attendance problems; bullying; repeated inappropriate language; deliberate minor damage to property; fighting; repeated breaches of School rules; or other behaviour requiring intervention beyond ordinary classroom management.

Such matters may be referred to the Head Teacher or designated School authority.

Level 3 — Serious Misconduct

Serious misconduct may include behaviour such as: serious violence; serious threats; possession or use of prohibited dangerous items; serious bullying or intimidation; serious sexual misconduct; serious harassment; deliberate serious damage to property; theft; serious acts of dishonesty; serious threats to School safety; serious abuse of another person; or other conduct considered sufficiently serious to require immediate intervention.

Serious incidents must be referred promptly to the Head Teacher. Where required, the matter should also be referred to the appropriate external authority.

11. Bullying and Harassment

Baimuru Primary School does not tolerate bullying, intimidation or harassment.

Bullying may involve repeated behaviour intended to hurt, intimidate, humiliate, exclude or otherwise harm another person. It may occur: physically; verbally; socially; psychologically; through property; through technology or electronic communication; or through other forms of interaction.

Students who experience or witness bullying are encouraged to report it to a teacher, Head Teacher or another trusted adult. Reports should be taken seriously and dealt with appropriately.

Students found responsible for bullying may be subject to behaviour-management responses appropriate to the seriousness and circumstances of the conduct.

12. Fighting and Violence

Violence and fighting are not acceptable forms of resolving disagreements.

Where a physical altercation occurs, the immediate priority is to:

  1. protect students and staff from further harm;
  2. separate those involved where it is safe to do so;
  3. obtain medical assistance where necessary;
  4. notify the appropriate School authority;
  5. establish the relevant facts;
  6. communicate with parents or guardians where appropriate; and
  7. determine an appropriate response.

Students should not be encouraged to retaliate or resolve disputes through violence.

13. Child Protection and Safeguarding

Behaviour-management processes must operate alongside the School's child-protection and safeguarding responsibilities.

Where behaviour raises concerns about abuse, neglect, exploitation, serious harm, sexual misconduct, domestic or family violence, or another child-protection matter, the School must follow its Child Protection and Safeguarding Policy and applicable reporting requirements.

A behaviour-management response must not be used to conceal or replace a required child-protection response.

14. Attendance and Truancy

Regular attendance is important to student learning.

The School will monitor attendance and respond to: repeated lateness; unexplained absence; persistent absenteeism; unauthorised absence; and truancy.

The School may work with parents, guardians and relevant authorities to understand and address persistent attendance problems.

Where attendance difficulties are related to welfare, family or other circumstances, the School should consider appropriate support rather than treating the matter solely as misconduct.

15. Prohibited Behaviour

The School prohibits behaviour that threatens the safety, dignity or orderly operation of the School. This may include:

  • violence;
  • threats;
  • bullying;
  • intimidation;
  • harassment;
  • theft;
  • deliberate serious property damage;
  • serious dishonesty;
  • possession of prohibited items;
  • sexual misconduct;
  • serious abuse;
  • deliberate actions that place others at serious risk; and
  • other behaviour prohibited under applicable laws, education requirements or School rules.

The specific list of prohibited behaviour may be expanded through the School's Student Code of Conduct and School Rules.

16. Disciplinary Responses

The School will use responses that are appropriate to the nature and seriousness of the behaviour.

Possible responses may include:

  • verbal reminder;
  • correction and redirection;
  • restorative conversation;
  • behaviour reflection;
  • additional guidance;
  • parent/guardian contact;
  • behaviour-support plan;
  • loss of an appropriate privilege;
  • supervised corrective activity;
  • formal warning;
  • formal behaviour meeting;
  • referral to the Head Teacher;
  • referral to the Board of Management where appropriate and within its authority; or
  • other lawful and authorised responses.

The response should consider: seriousness of the behaviour; frequency; student's age and developmental level; previous behaviour; circumstances surrounding the incident; impact on others; safety considerations; whether the student accepts responsibility; whether harm can be repaired; and any relevant welfare or safeguarding concerns.

17. Prohibited Disciplinary Practices

Behaviour management must not involve practices that are unlawful, degrading, abusive or that place students at unreasonable risk of harm.

The School will not tolerate:

  • physical abuse;
  • humiliating treatment;
  • degrading punishment;
  • threats of violence;
  • discriminatory treatment;
  • collective punishment where inappropriate;
  • retaliation against a student for making a genuine complaint or report; or
  • any disciplinary practice inconsistent with applicable law or education policy.

Discipline must remain educational, proportionate, respectful and focused on student welfare and safety.

18. Restorative Approach

Where appropriate, the School will encourage students to understand: what happened; why the behaviour was inappropriate; who was affected; what responsibility they have; how harm can be repaired; and what they can do differently in future.

Restorative approaches may include: guided discussion; apology where appropriate; repairing or replacing damaged property; restoring relationships; behaviour agreements; and follow-up support.

Restorative approaches should not replace immediate protective action where safety is at risk.

19. Serious Incident Response

Where a serious incident occurs, the School should follow an appropriate incident-response process.

The immediate priorities are:

  1. safety;
  2. medical assistance where necessary;
  3. protection of affected students and staff;
  4. notification of the Head Teacher;
  5. appropriate communication with parents or guardians;
  6. documentation;
  7. investigation or fact-finding;
  8. appropriate disciplinary or welfare response; and
  9. referral to external authorities where required.

Serious incidents must be handled confidentially and professionally.

20. Investigation and Fair Process

Where a student may face a significant disciplinary consequence, the School should provide an appropriate opportunity for the relevant circumstances to be considered.

The School should: establish the relevant facts; allow the student to explain their perspective; consider available evidence; avoid prejudging the matter; consider relevant circumstances; maintain appropriate confidentiality; and make a reasonable and proportionate decision.

The extent of the process will depend on the seriousness of the matter.

21. Parent and Guardian Involvement

Parents or guardians may be contacted when: behaviour is repeated; behaviour is serious; a student's safety or welfare is affected; a formal intervention is required; the School requires additional information; or cooperation is needed to support improvement.

The School will seek to work collaboratively with parents and guardians rather than treating behaviour management solely as a disciplinary matter.

22. Behaviour Support Plans

Where a student's behaviour is persistent or requires additional support, the School may develop an individual behaviour-support plan.

A behaviour-support plan may identify: the behaviour of concern; possible contributing factors; desired behaviour; support strategies; responsibilities; monitoring arrangements; communication arrangements; and review dates.

The purpose is to help the student succeed rather than simply to increase punishment.

23. Confidentiality

Behaviour and disciplinary information must be handled appropriately.

Information concerning a student's behaviour should only be shared with persons who have a legitimate reason to receive it.

The School will seek to protect the dignity and privacy of students, families and staff while meeting its legal, safeguarding and administrative responsibilities.

24. Record Keeping

Significant behaviour incidents should be appropriately documented.

Records may include: incident reports; behaviour records; disciplinary records; parent communication records; behaviour-support plans; meeting records; referrals; and follow-up records.

Records should be accurate, factual, confidential and securely maintained.

25. Appeals and Complaints

Students and parents or guardians who have concerns about a behaviour-management decision should be able to raise those concerns through the appropriate School process.

Complaints should be: received respectfully; considered fairly; handled confidentially where appropriate; documented where necessary; and referred to the appropriate authority where the matter exceeds the School's authority.

Nothing in this policy prevents a person from exercising a right of complaint or appeal provided under applicable law or education requirements.

26. Behaviour Management Committee

Where considered necessary, the School may establish a Behaviour Management Committee or designate appropriate staff to assist with significant behaviour matters.

The composition and responsibilities of such a committee should be determined by the School and remain within the authority of the School's governance arrangements.

A committee should support, rather than replace, the responsibilities of the Head Teacher and governing body.

27. Training and Professional Development

The School should support teachers and relevant staff to develop appropriate skills in: positive behaviour management; classroom management; conflict resolution; child protection; student welfare; restorative approaches; incident management; and appropriate referral processes.

Professional development should be considered as part of the School's broader staff-development planning.

28. Monitoring and Review

The Head Teacher and School administration will monitor the implementation of this policy.

Monitoring may consider: frequency of behaviour incidents; types of incidents; repeated behaviour; attendance concerns; bullying reports; serious incidents; disciplinary responses; student welfare concerns; parent feedback; and effectiveness of behaviour-support strategies.

The policy should be reviewed periodically and whenever significant changes occur in relevant legislation, national education policy, provincial requirements or School circumstances.

29. Related Documents

This policy should be read together with the following School documents:

  • Baimuru Primary School Constitution;
  • Governance and Administrative Policies;
  • Student Code of Conduct;
  • School Rules;
  • Child Protection and Safeguarding Policy;
  • Student Administration and Welfare Policy;
  • Attendance Procedure;
  • Behaviour and Discipline Procedure;
  • Serious Incident Response Procedure;
  • Child Protection Reporting Procedure;
  • Student Records Procedure; and
  • relevant forms, registers and records.

30. Policy Implementation

The implementation of this policy is the responsibility of the School leadership, teachers, staff, students and wider School community within their respective responsibilities.

The Head Teacher will coordinate implementation and provide appropriate guidance to staff. Teachers will implement appropriate classroom behaviour-management practices. Students will be expected to understand and follow the School's behaviour expectations. Parents and guardians will be encouraged to support the School in promoting positive behaviour.

31. Policy Review and Approval

This policy shall be reviewed periodically to ensure that it remains: consistent with applicable PNG legislation; aligned with national and provincial education requirements; appropriate to the circumstances of Baimuru Primary School; practical for teachers and students; supportive of student welfare; and effective in promoting positive behaviour.
